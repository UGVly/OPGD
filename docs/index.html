One-Step RL-Guided On-Policy Distillation

On-policy distillation (OPD) offers dense supervision for reasoning models, but its use with reward-trained experts commonly follows a train-then-distill workflow. We show that effective guidance can instead come from a teacher only one reward-gradient step ahead of the student. Our method, On-Policy Gradient Distillation (OPGD), rebuilds this teacher from the current actor at every iteration, allowing it to inherit accumulated learning while adapting to fresh verified outcomes. The teacher–actor log ratio isolates the preferences induced by this adaptation and supplies signed token advantages for OPD on the same rollouts. A first-order analysis connects these preferences to interactions between token score gradients, explaining how response-level feedback is redistributed within and across responses. Distillation reweights the outcome gradient through the policy's local geometry while preserving nonnegative first-order alignment. On Qwen3-4B, OPGD improves mean Pass@1 over GRPO by 9.2 points across four mathematical benchmarks at a common 100-update budget, and training on coding prompts yields faster learning and higher accuracy across three code-generation benchmarks.

How OPGD Works

Each training step reuses a single rollout batch for two jobs — construct the teacher, then distill it back into the actor. Given group-normalized (GRPO) advantages, the outcome gradient is

Outcome gradient
$$A_i = \frac{R_i - \bar{R}_{q_i}}{\sigma_{q_i} + \varepsilon}, \qquad g_k = \sum_{(i,t)\in K} w_{it}\, A_i\, s_{it}, \quad s_{it} = \nabla_\theta \log \pi_{\theta_k}(x_{it}\mid c_{it}).$$

A virtual teacher is then formed with one stateless SGD step of lookahead rate $\alpha$, independent of the actor's learning rate:

One-step teacher
$$\theta^E_k = \theta_k + \alpha\, g_k.$$

The teacher–student log ratio on the sampled tokens becomes a signed token-level OPD advantage, and the actor is optimized with these detached advantages:

Token advantage & objective
$$d_{it} = \log \pi_{\theta^E_k}(x_{it}\mid c_{it}) - \log \pi_{\theta_k}(x_{it}\mid c_{it}), \qquad L_{\mathrm{OPGD}}(\theta) = -\sum_{i,t} v_{it}\,\mathrm{sg}[d_{it}]\,\log \pi_\theta(x_{it}\mid c_{it}).$$
Algorithm 1 — On-Policy Gradient Distillation (OPGD)
Require: actor $\theta_k$, verifier $R$, prompt batch, group size $G$, lookahead rate $\alpha$, retained fraction $f$
  1. Sample $G$ responses per prompt from $\pi_{\theta_k}$ and evaluate their rewards.
  2. Compute group advantages $A_i$ and the outcome gradient $g_k$.
  3. Save actor parameters $\theta_k$ and sampled-token log probabilities $\ell_{it}^{0}$.
  4. Apply the stateless SGD step $\theta \leftarrow \theta_k + \alpha\, g_k$.
  5. Measure $d_{it} \leftarrow \mathrm{sg}\!\big[\log \pi_\theta(x_{it}\mid c_{it}) - \ell_{it}^{0}\big]$.
  6. Restore $\theta \leftarrow \theta_k$ and clear temporary gradients.
  7. Retain the top fraction $f$ of $|d_{it}|$ within each response ($f=1$ keeps all tokens).
  8. Update the actor once and advance its optimizer and scheduler.

Why One Step Is Enough

Linearizing the teacher gives an NTK view of contextual credit: a token's coefficient sums the batch outcome signal over its score similarities with every other sampled token.

Contextual credit
$$d_{it} \approx \alpha\, s_{it}^\top g_k = \alpha \sum_{j,u} w_{ju} A_j\, \underbrace{s_{it}^\top s_{ju}}_{K(it,ju)}.$$

The diagonal contribution carries the sign of the token's own response advantage, but interactions with other tokens can reinforce or reverse it. This is why the same token can receive opposite advantages in different contexts, and why responses with zero group advantage still receive a signal from the rest of the batch:

The induced update reweights the outcome gradient through the actor's local geometry while preserving nonnegative first-order alignment:

Geometry & alignment
$$h_k = -\nabla_\theta L_{\mathrm{OPGD}}(\theta_k) \approx \alpha F_k g_k, \qquad F_k = \sum_{i,t} v_{it}\, s_{it} s_{it}^\top, \qquad g_k^\top h_k \approx \tfrac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{i,t} v_{it} d_{it}^2 \ge 0.$$

Because every position contributes proportionally to $v_{it} d_{it}^2$, retaining the largest $|d_{it}|$ per response maximizes the alignment — motivating the top-10% selection variant. The checked-in configuration keeps all tokens ($f=1$).

Gradient Structure

At the same actor and rollout batch, matching the teacher's preferences concentrates singular-value energy in the leading gradient directions and lowers spectral entropy across layer groups — most strongly in later layers and the embedding.
